> **Synthetic local-test fixture.** This document is fictional and its database approval is a seeded test state, not evidence of review by real Northstar personnel. It is written to test ingestion, retrieval, citations, and teaching flows, not to direct work on real equipment. It does not authorize a physical calibration, inspection, or repair. A real organization would need its own technical validation and approval before operational use. If a current work instruction cannot be verified, stop at the information boundary and ask an authorized reviewer; do not infer a setting or action from this document.

## 1. Fundamentals

The purpose of this SOP is to keep three questions separate during calibration-related work: **Which instruction is currently authorized? What is actually known about this work item? What needs clarification before a decision can be made?** A plausible past incident or familiar procedure is not a substitute for a current approved instruction. The work system, source document, and human reviewer each provide different kinds of evidence; none should be silently promoted into another's authority.

### 1. Find the current procedure

#### 1.1 When this section applies

Use this section when preparing for a calibration-related task, answering a coworker's procedure question, onboarding a new team member, or handing work to another shift or owner. It is a **document-selection workflow**, not a calibration workflow. It helps identify the right instruction before any operational step is considered.

If the task is urgent, safety-critical, or outside the employee's authorization, notify the designated lead or safety authority under the organization's actual escalation process. This fixture does not define that process. Do not improvise physical actions while looking for documentation.

#### 1.2 Resolve the source in order

1. Identify the organization, work area or team, asset, and work item. Record the exact asset and ticket identifiers; a similar title is not enough to establish that two records describe the same work.
2. Open the organization's controlled document record for the relevant procedure. Verify its document code, version, approval status, effective status, and the audience permitted to use it.
3. Confirm that the selected version is **approved and current** for the work context. A downloaded copy, old bookmark, prior shift attachment, or search hit may be historical even when its content looks useful.
4. Match the document's scope to the question. A source about evidence handling cannot supply a calibration parameter, repair step, or acceptance limit. If the needed instruction is in another controlled document, locate and verify that document separately.
5. Capture the precise source reference used in the work record: document code, version, section heading, and, where available, the stable source link or identifier.

In the Northstar test scenario after the v3.4 fixture is activated, SOP-001 v3.4 is approved/current; v3.3 and v3.2 remain approved but superseded history. The status of a version is determined by the controlled registry, not by which Markdown file happens to be newest on disk.

#### 1.3 If the source is missing or conflicting

Treat a missing, inaccessible, expired, or contradictory instruction as a document-control gap. Record what was searched and the versions found. Ask the authorized document owner or supervisor to identify the applicable instruction. Do not decide that a superseded version is safe because it resembles the current task. Do not have an AI assistant fill in a missing numeric value or operational step.

If two approved/current entries appear to conflict, preserve both references and escalate the discrepancy. The reviewer must resolve the version or scope conflict before a consequential step depends on it. A work item may remain open while the document issue is investigated.

#### 1.4 Evidence of correct source selection

A useful source-selection note contains the work item ID, asset ID, approved document code and version, relevant section, the lookup time, and any limitation. For example: “For Ticket #829 on Machine #14, SOP-001 v3.4 is the current document-control reference in this synthetic test workspace. It does not state a vibration cause or a coolant-pressure target. The applicable machine-operation instruction still needs confirmation.” This is a **training example**, not a new live observation or operating instruction.

### 2. Separate history from evidence

#### 2.1 Use explicit evidence labels

Every statement added to a work update should be recognizable as one of the following:

| Label | Meaning | Example wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reported | Someone described an observation or action; it has not necessarily been independently verified | “Alex reported abnormal vibration.” |
| Verified | An authorized person or system confirmed a specific fact, with a traceable source | “Reviewer confirmed the ticket is still open.” |
| Historical | The fact belongs to an earlier event or superseded document | “Ticket #492 recorded a loose mounting assembly in that earlier incident.” |
| Unknown | The available evidence does not establish the answer | “The cause of today's vibration is unknown.” |
| Proposed | A next step, hypothesis, or AI suggestion awaits human review | “Review the open ticket against the current authorized procedure.” |

These labels describe the status of a **specific claim**, not the overall quality of an employee. A report can be valuable without being a diagnosis. Verification of one field does not verify every related claim. A confirmed historical cause remains historical when a similar symptom appears again.

#### 2.2 Record the minimum useful work context

For each substantive work event, capture the exact work item and asset identifiers, who made the report, when it was recorded, what was observed or completed, the claim's evidence label, the source reference, what remains unresolved, and who is expected to review or act next. Keep the event timestamp separate from the time an observation allegedly occurred; if the latter is unknown, leave it unknown. Do not backfill a precise time from the time a chat message was saved.

Avoid merging several actions into an unsupported summary. “Steps 1–4 were reported complete” does not mean that the complete inspection passed, that a cause was found, or that the ticket can be closed. A work event can inform a handover suggestion without automatically modifying the published handover or changing the official work status.

#### 2.3 Compare prior cases without copying conclusions

Historical incidents can suggest questions to ask, records to inspect, or expertise to seek. They do not prove a current cause. In the seeded training scenario, Ticket #492 is a closed prior Machine #14 vibration incident whose recorded cause was a loose mounting assembly. Ticket #829 is a separate open Machine #14 inspection with abnormal vibration reported and current cause **unknown**. The only safe connection is that #492 may be labeled as historical context for review; its diagnosis must not be asserted as the diagnosis of #829.

Similarly, Machine #22 and Ticket #910 are a separate document-reference check. Neither is evidence that Machine #14 has a particular condition. Keep asset and work-item boundaries intact even when a search result mentions related terminology.

#### 2.4 Make uncertainty visible at handover and transfer

An outgoing employee should separate four things: the current approved procedure; the present status of open work; historical context; and unresolved questions. A shift handover transfers awareness and follow-up to the next shift. A role-transition package helps a successor assume an ongoing responsibility after authorization. Neither artifact should silently convert a reported observation into verified fact or close an open ticket.

If an AI system proposes a missing handover item or a status correction based on a member report, the shift lead reviews its source and accepts or rejects the suggestion. The suggestion remains distinct from the official published handover until accepted and published. If a later shift reports completion, distinguish reported completion, lead confirmation, source-shift acknowledgement, and closure.

#### 2.5 Evidence-quality check

Before sharing a summary, ask: Can a reader locate the exact source and version? Is the claim tied to the right asset and work item? Does “reported” remain reported? Are the current and historical events visibly different? Are unknowns still visible? Has any private interview or draft knowledge artifact been presented as approved organizational knowledge? If any answer is uncertain, correct the summary or ask for review.

### 3. Ask a clearer question

#### 3.1 When clarification is needed

Ask for clarification when the approved source is missing, out of scope, ambiguous, inaccessible, or contradicted by another source; when an observation cannot support the proposed conclusion; when the actor lacks authority to approve or publish a change; or when a work item lacks the evidence needed for the next decision. A good question narrows the uncertainty instead of asking someone to reconstruct the entire case.

Do not ask an AI assistant to invent a company-specific procedure or to choose a physical setting from general knowledge. AI may help draft the question and show cited current material. The authorized human owner supplies or approves the missing instruction or decision.

#### 3.2 Build the question from five parts

1. **Context:** State the organization, asset, work item, and relevant role or shift.
2. **Current source:** Give the approved document code, version, and section consulted, or say that no applicable approved source was found.
3. **Known evidence:** Distinguish reported facts, verified facts, and historical context; include citations or record IDs.
4. **Exact gap:** Name the missing instruction, contradictory statement, unverified observation, or authority decision.
5. **Requested response:** Ask a specific reviewer to identify the controlling source, validate a fact, approve a revision, or assign an owner. Include the required response time only if a real policy or deadline establishes one.

Question template:

> For **[work item]** on **[asset]**, I checked **[approved document/version/section]**. **[Reported or verified fact, with source]**. **[Historical context, if relevant and clearly labeled]**. I cannot verify **[specific unknown]** from the available approved material. Could **[authorized reviewer]** confirm **[specific decision or source]**? Until then, I will keep **[status/claim]** marked **[open/unknown/pending]**.

This template is for communication only. It does not authorize delaying a safety escalation; an actual emergency policy, where one exists, governs urgent action.

#### 3.3 Worked training example

Weak question: “Is the vibration caused by the same thing as before?” It hides which ticket is current, which event is historical, and what evidence is missing.

Clearer question: “For open Ticket #829 on Machine #14, I checked the current SOP-001 v3.4 evidence-handling reference in this synthetic test workspace. Abnormal vibration was reported; today's cause is not verified. Ticket #492 recorded a loose mounting assembly in a prior incident, but that is historical context only. Which authorized inspection record or reviewer can establish the current cause, and should #829 remain open while that evidence is gathered?”

This example reflects the **synthetic training state after the v3.4 fixture is applied**. It is not a new ticket update, a diagnosis, or a physical inspection instruction.

#### 3.4 Close the information loop

When a reviewer answers, record who responded, the source or decision they supplied, any effective version or time, and what remains open. Update the work item's authoritative state only through the permitted workflow. If the response changes a controlled document, submit a proposed revision and obtain approval of the exact content before indexing it as current knowledge. A private knowledge-transfer interview or draft checklist is not automatically publishable SOP content.

If the answer remains incomplete, preserve the open question and assign a follow-up owner. “Asked” is not the same as “resolved.” A later shift or successor should see both the question and the current status without needing to infer them from chat history.

## 2. Reference aids

### A. Source-selection checklist

- [ ] Correct organization, asset, and work item identified.
- [ ] Controlled document record opened, not only a copied excerpt or search snippet.
- [ ] Document code, version, approval status, effective status, scope, and audience checked.
- [ ] Relevant section cited; any missing operational instruction identified explicitly.
- [ ] Superseded and draft versions excluded from current operational guidance.
- [ ] Any discrepancy or unavailable source sent to an authorized reviewer.

### B. Work-evidence note template

| Field | Entry guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Work item / asset | Exact identifiers, not an approximate title |
| Recorder / recorded at | Person and system timestamp |
| Observation time | Separate timestamp, or “not known” |
| Claim | Concise statement without inferred cause |
| Evidence status | Reported, verified, historical, unknown, or proposed |
| Source | Ticket event, document version/section, or approved external record |
| Remaining gap | What is not established |
| Reviewer / next owner | Authorized person or team for follow-up |

### C. Training checks

1. A search returns SOP-001 v3.2 or v3.3 above v3.4 because its wording matches the query. Which version supplies current guidance, and why? Expected reasoning: retrieval rank does not override approved/current status; v3.4 is current after the synthetic test fixture is activated.
2. A prior Machine #14 ticket has a documented cause, while today's ticket has a similar symptom. What can be carried forward? Expected reasoning: cite the prior case as historical context, not as proof of today's cause.
3. A coworker asks for the coolant-pressure target, but the approved evidence-handling fixture contains no target. What should be said? Expected reasoning: the value is unavailable from this source; locate the applicable approved operational instruction or authorized reviewer. Do not invent a number.
4. A member reports completing part of an inspection. What may a shift lead put in the handover? Expected reasoning: preserve the report and source, review any suggested status change, and publish only the lead-approved snapshot. A partial report does not automatically close the underlying ticket.

These checks support onboarding discussion. They are not standalone proof of mastery; the curriculum's required objectives and assessment evidence control progression.

### D. Version and publication boundary

This version is intentionally separate from the previously seeded approved versions. The local SQL fixture creates a new `knowledge_document_versions` row with a new version ID, synthetic reviewer metadata, and a distinct source record; the previous approved version remains historical. The ingestion code should generate chunks and embeddings for this **exact approved synthetic revision** without overwriting old content or chunks. Search eligibility must still follow approval, temporal status, tenant/workspace permissions, and index readiness. A later content change requires another version rather than an in-place edit.
