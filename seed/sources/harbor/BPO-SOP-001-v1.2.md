> **Synthetic test procedure.** This document is fictional. It describes how a Harbor support queue handles customer account cases in the WorkWeave demo. It does not define real refund rules, identity checks or authority over a customer's account. Decisions about money stay with the authorized billing reviewer.

## 1. Fundamentals

This guide keeps three things apart on every case: **which guide is currently approved, what the customer told us versus what we verified, and who may see and decide on the case.** A colleague's memory, an old saved copy or a similar past case is not a substitute for the approved guide and the account record.

### 1. Use the approved account guide

#### 1.1 What the account guide is for

The account guide is the queue's approved reference for handling customer account cases: what to check, what to record, and who decides. Use it when you take a case, when you answer a teammate's question about how a case is handled, and when you hand a case to another shift.

#### 1.2 Find the current version

1. Open the document library and search by the document code (BPO-SOP-001, BPO-PRC-004), not by a title you remember.
2. Use only the version marked **approved** and **current**. Older versions stay visible as history, but they are not instructions.
3. If you have a saved or printed copy, compare its version with the library before relying on it. A copy without a version is treated as unknown.
4. Record the code and version you used in the case, for example "BPO-SOP-001 v1.2".

#### 1.3 When the guide doesn't cover the case

If the guide has no answer, or two documents disagree, do not improvise a rule for the customer. Record what you looked for, keep both references, and ask the document owner or your shift lead. Tell the customer you are confirming the right process and when they will hear back.

#### 1.4 Roles

- **Support agent:** works the case, records facts, explains outcomes the record supports.
- **Shift lead:** decides when the guide is unclear and reviews escalations.
- **Billing reviewer:** the only person who decides on changes to charges.
- **Document owner:** answers questions about which guide applies.

### 2. Separate customer reports from verified facts

#### 2.1 Label every statement

Every statement in a case note carries one label:

| Label | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verified** | You checked it in the account record, with the reference | "Charge of 14 Oct, reference INV-2209, is on the account" |
| **Reported** | The customer or a colleague told you; not yet checked | "Customer says a discount was promised by phone" |
| **Unknown** | Not established yet | "Whether the discount was approved: unknown" |

#### 2.2 Record the customer's account in their terms

Write what the customer says happened in their words and label it **reported**: what they expected, what they saw, when, and why they think it is wrong. Do not write "billing error", "overcharge" or "our mistake" until it is verified.

#### 2.3 Check the account record

Compare the customer's report with the account record: the charge, its reference and date, and the plan or agreement on the account. Record each point as matches, does not match, or unknown. If something cannot be checked in the record, ask the customer for what they have (a reference number or a message) and record it as reported evidence.

#### 2.4 Past cases are not evidence

A similar past case can tell you where to look, never what happened this time. "Last month's dispute was a duplicate charge" is a lead to check in this account's record, not a conclusion for this case.

### 3. Escalate without exposing private data

#### 3.1 When to escalate

Escalate when the record shows a mismatch you are not authorized to fix, when the customer asks for something outside your authority (for example a refund or a credit), when the guide doesn't cover the case, or when the customer is a priority account with a missed response target.

#### 3.2 What an escalation contains

An escalation names:

1. The case ID and the guide version you followed.
2. What is verified, with record references.
3. What the customer reported.
4. What is still unknown, and who can answer it.
5. What decision you need, and from whom.

#### 3.3 Share only what is needed

Share only what the reviewer needs for this case. Do not paste full payment details, identity-check answers, other customers' information or notes from unrelated cases into the escalation, the team chat or a handover. Refer to the record by its reference instead of copying it. If someone without access asks about the case, tell them who owns it instead of sharing details.

#### 3.4 Keep the customer informed

Tell the customer the case has gone to the right person and when they can expect an update. Do not promise an outcome you cannot authorize. Record what you told them in the case, then keep the case **in progress** or mark it **blocked** in the case system while you wait for the decision.

## 2. Reference aids

### A. Case note checklist

- [ ] Guide code and version recorded (for example BPO-SOP-001 v1.2).
- [ ] Customer's account recorded in their terms, labelled reported.
- [ ] Record checks listed as matches, does not match or unknown, with references.
- [ ] Unknowns listed, with who can answer them.
- [ ] No private data beyond what the case needs.
- [ ] Status updated in the case system, not only in chat.

### B. Escalation template

| Field | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Case | Case ID and short title |
| Guide | Code and version followed |
| Verified | Facts from the account record, with references |
| Reported | What the customer said |
| Unknown | What is missing and who can provide it |
| Decision needed | What you need decided, and by whom |
